## Abstract

This study examines the effectiveness of "Fiscales Escolares," a school-based crime prevention program, in reducing low-level drug trade, property crimes, and physical injuries near secondary schools, as well as its impact on school violence and dropout rates. Using a two-way fixed-effects difference-in-differences (TWFE DiD) approach, the analysis also explores heterogeneous effects in high-crime districts. Sustained program implementation significantly reduces physical injuries by 2.1% and dropout rates by 0.9%, though it is associated with a modest increase of 0.3% in reported student violence. The results also show a reduction in the share of detained teenagers charged with homicide. Effects on property crimes and low-level drug trade are not statistically significant.
