M.A. Economist at the University of British Columbia and B.A. in Economics from the Pontifical Catholic University of Peru (PUCP), specializing in causal inference, applied econometrics, and data science.

My work focuses on combining economic theory with modern machine learning methods. I have experience developing end-to-end Python pipelines for ETL processes, geospatial analytics, and extracting economic insights from remote sensing data. In addition to research, I have served as a teaching assistant for quantitative courses including econometrics, applied statistics, R and Python programming, and microeconomics.

I am interested in the intersection of economics, machine learning, deep learning, and generative AI for business problems and applied economics. My Master's thesis uses quasi-experimental econometric models and machine-learning-based estimators to show that the El Niño climate shock reduced firm-level productivity by 12%, with the effect persisting for three years after the shock.

I have also completed a prototype chatbot on Telegram, @IndecopiChatbot: a virtual assistant that guides users of the Peruvian banking system about their rights as consumers against financial fraud, based on real INDECOPI resolutions (Instituto Nacional de Defensa de la Competencia y de la Protección de la Propiedad Intelectual — the Peruvian consumer protection authority). It is available as a Telegram bot (@IndecopiChatbot) and as a REST API.

My ongoing project uses convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and regularization to extract features from daytime satellite imagery and integrates them with socioeconomic data to predict poverty at a granular spatial level (1 km × 1 km grid cells).
